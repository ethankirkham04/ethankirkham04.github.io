AI Security is a project focused on testing the security and reliability of an AI tutor system used for educational purposes.  The system is designed to help students by answering questions, explaining course material, and guiding them through problems.  For this project, our team tested different ways the AI tutor could be manipulated.  One group focused on RAG poisoning, where incorrect information was placed into course materials to see if the AI would retrieve and use it.  Another group focused on jailbreaking, where different prompts were used to try to bypass the AI tutor's safety rules and academic integrity protections.  The goal of the project was to find weaknesses in the system and test ways to make the AI tutor more secure and reliable.

For this project, I was part of the jailbreaking team and was responsible for creating and testing different prompts against the AI tutor.  I tested techniques such as roleplay prompts, authority framing, indirect prompting, prompt injection, and multi-step conversations.  From there, I changed the wording and structure of the prompts to see how the AI's responses would change and whether it would reveal restricted information or give answers it was not supposed to provide.  I also helped test updated defense prompts to see if the AI tutor could better recognize and reject jailbreak attempts.  We found that small changes in wording and conversational context could greatly affect the AI's behavior, and although the updated defenses improved the system, some jailbreak attempts were still partially successful.
